Bully Algorithm

1. What is Bully Algorithm?
Answer:
Bully Algorithm is a distributed leader election algorithm used to elect a coordinator in distributed systems.
The process with the highest process ID becomes the coordinator.

2. Why is it called Bully Algorithm?
Answer:
It is called Bully Algorithm because the process with the highest priority (highest ID) dominates all lower-priority processes and becomes the leader.

3. What is the main objective of Bully Algorithm?
Answer:
Elect a Coordinator in Distributed Systems
when the current coordinator fails.
Complete Step-by-Step Working of Bully Algorithm

Step 1 — Initial State
All processes have:
unique IDs,
knowledge about other process IDs.
Example:
P1 P2 P3 P4 P5
Highest process ID acts as coordinator.
Example:
P5 = Coordinator

Step 2 — Coordinator Failure
Suppose:
P5 crashes
Another process detects coordinator failure.
Example:
P2 detects failure

Step 3 — Election Starts
P2 sends:
Election Messages
to all higher-ID processes:
P3 P4 P5

Step 4 — Higher Processes Reply
Alive higher processes send:
OK Message
Meaning:
"I am alive and will take over election."
Example:
P3 → OK
P4 → OK

Step 5 — Higher Process Starts New Election
Now P4 starts its own election and sends messages to:
P5
But P5 is crashed.

Step 6 — No Higher Response
Since no higher process responds:
P4 becomes coordinator

Step 7 — Coordinator Announcement
P4 sends:
Coordinator Message
to all processes.
Now:
P4 = New Coordinator
Final Working Flow
Coordinator crashes
        ↓
Lower process detects failure
        ↓
Election messages sent to higher processes
        ↓
Higher alive process replies OK
        ↓
Highest alive process becomes coordinator
        ↓
Coordinator message broadcasted

4. What is Election Message?
Answer:
Election message is sent to higher-ID processes to check whether they are alive.

5. What is OK Message?
Answer:
OK message indicates:
"I am alive and have higher priority."

6. Which process becomes coordinator?
Answer:
Highest Alive Process ID
becomes coordinator.

7. Who initiates election?
Answer:
The process that detects coordinator failure initiates election.

8. What happens if no higher process responds?
Answer:
The current process declares itself as coordinator.

9. What is the time complexity of Bully Algorithm?
Answer:
O(n2)
because many election and response messages may be exchanged.

10. Why is time complexity high in Bully Algorithm?
Answer:
Because multiple processes may independently start elections and exchange many messages.

11. What are the advantages of Bully Algorithm?
Answer:
Fast coordinator selection
Simple implementation
Highest priority process becomes leader

12. What are the disadvantages of Bully Algorithm?
Answer:
Large number of messages
High communication overhead
Inefficient for large systems

13. What happens if the coordinator crashes again?
Answer:
Another election process starts automatically.

14. What assumptions does Bully Algorithm make?
Answer:
Every process has unique ID
Every process knows all other process IDs
Processes can communicate directly

15. Is Bully Algorithm centralized or decentralized?
Answer:
Election process is decentralized, but finally one centralized coordinator is elected.

16. Why does highest process ID become coordinator?
Answer:
Higher ID represents higher priority and authority.

17. What is coordinator in distributed systems?
Answer:
Coordinator is the leader process responsible for managing distributed activities.

18. Can two coordinators exist simultaneously?
Answer:
No.
Bully Algorithm ensures only one highest alive process becomes coordinator.

19. Why is Bully Algorithm important?
Answer:
It ensures fault tolerance by electing a new coordinator when the current one fails.

20. Applications of Bully Algorithm?
Answer:
Distributed databases
Cluster management
Distributed operating systems
Resource allocation systems
Fault-tolerant systems
